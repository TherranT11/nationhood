Three offices hold the nation.
None holds it alone.

You don't play a country. You play one faction inside it — the Government, the Labour bloc, or the Military — alongside two other people who want what you want and won't give it to you.

Budgets are voted, not assigned. Wars need a treasury the Government controls, factories the Labour bloc can stop, and officers the Military won't lend for nothing. Fall out badly enough and the nation falls with you.

The wire

Choose the office, not the country

Each nation seats exactly three players. Pick what you want to control — the country you get assigned to comes second.

G

The Government

Treasury · Law · Foreign office

You raise the money and you sign the treaties. Nothing the other two want happens without a budget line, and every budget line you refuse buys you an enemy at home.

  • Sets taxation and tariffsBoth other offices are paid out of what you collect.
  • Speaks to foreign powersOnly you can open talks, sign, or break a treaty.
  • Can be brought downLose three divisions in a row and your ministry falls.

31 seats open

L

The Labour Bloc

Choose one course at founding

You are the country's capacity to make things — and to stop making them. Nothing gets built, shipped, or armed on a timetable you refuse to keep.

  • The MovementOrganise, strike, and push reform. Cheap power, slow power, and it frightens the Cabinet.
  • The IndustrialistsOwn the mills and the yards. Fast output, private profit, and the streets turn on you.
  • You cannot be bothThe course is fixed when you take the seat.

44 seats open

M

The Military

Choose one service at founding

You hold the only force in the country, and you are the only office that can lose the whole game in an afternoon. You do not control what you're paid.

  • The ArmyBorders, garrisons, and the capital. Cheap to raise, ruinous to keep standing.
  • The NavyTrade lanes, colonies, and blockade. Slow to build, and it decides who eats.
  • You cannot be bothThe service is fixed when you take the seat.

A day in session

The world runs whether you're at the desk or not. Four fixed hours, every day, world time.

07.00

Dispatches

The night's events arrive: harvests, strikes, ships sighted, what the neighbours did.

12.00

Debate

Open floor. Table motions, trade favours, threaten to walk. Nothing binds yet.

18.00

Division

All three offices vote. Two carry a motion. One can abstain and let it pass.

00.00

Consequences

Orders execute. Money moves, ships sail, unrest is counted, and it is on the record.
