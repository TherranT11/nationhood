✦  About the game

You are one personin a very large argument.

Two kingdoms are at war. A church is watching both of them. The people who farm the ground do not much care who wins. And somewhere on the map, in a city nobody visits twice, something is counting down to a night that does not end.

I  The shape of it

One character. One life. No empire to hide behind.

Most games of this size hand you a kingdom. This one hands you a person who owns almost nothing. You begin with no reputation, no followers, and no skills, and the only thing you have that anyone else wants is your time.

You win on Prestige, and Prestige comes from finishing quests. Everything else in the game exists to decide which quests you are allowed to attempt, and whether you survive them. Your first two are given to you at the table, chosen by your station, and they are small on purpose. Nobody starts important.

II  Standing

Five powers keep count of what you owe them.

Every one of them holds you somewhere between hunted and exalted. You can move on any track. You cannot move up all five at once, because most of what earns you one costs you another.

Shown: a Sessau chevalier, seventh season. Adored at court. Hated across the border. Tolerated by the church, resented by the villages she rode through to get here, and unknown to the thing under the city.

Standing is not a bonus. It is a door. At Honored with a crown you can call banners you do not pay for. At Despised no village in the province will follow you anywhere. At Hunted by the church, killing you is worth Prestige to every other player at the table.

Six things you know how to do. You get two.

IV  What you are made of

Five numbers, none above three when you begin.

Vigor
Hard roads, long sieges, poison, and how much of a wound you can walk off.
Presence
Being listened to. Being followed. Holding a room that did not want you in it.
Resolve
Fear, torture, temptation, and whatever it is that whispers to people at the edge of the map.
Prowess
Your own hands. Duels, the hunt, and the part of a battle where you are personally involved.
Cunning
Lying well, and noticing when someone else is. Low Cunning means you will never see it coming.

Stats rise slowly and cap low. Skills carry the detail: they are permanent, specific, and they change how a stat applies. Duellist sharpens your Prowess in single combat and nowhere else. Court Tongue lets your Presence work on Sessau nobility even when they cannot stand you.

V  Where you are from

Three cultures. Only two of them announce themselves.

Andric
Chainmail and obligation

Heraldry, oaths, and cold weather. Andrics are hard to kill and bad at subtlety. You start as a lowborn lord: a little land, a title nobody honors, and levies you cannot afford to keep for long.

Sessau
Court, glory, the knife after

Powdered wigs, patronage, and duels fought for an audience. Sessauans arrive already famous and entirely broke. You start as a chevalier: renown on a clock, and nothing underneath it yet.

Nightcourt
One city, no allies, no hurry

They hold a single settlement on the whole map and they are in no rush. You start as a vampire lord or a necromancer, hidden, at zero standing everywhere because nobody yet knows what you are. Your game is not conquest. It is getting away with it.

VI  The four chairs

Every throne is already occupied.

A crown is not the finish line. It is a very good engine bolted to a very public address. Kings can be deposed, and deposing one pays.

King of Andren
By right, or by force
Bring a claim, or declare yourself a usurper and make the claim yourself. Then survive a succession crisis while every other player quietly counts swords for your rival.
King of Sessau
By court and by glory
Won at parties, not battles. You need standing, renown, and the nerve to sit through a season of intrigue while rivals spend their own reputation trying to embarrass you out of the running.
Head of the Church
By acclaim, and a clean record
The hardest seat, because it audits your whole game. Sink low enough with the commonfolk, or take a single favor from the Nightcourt, and it is remembered when the votes are counted.
Lord of the Nightcourt
By succession, in person
Nobody elects you. You take it from the one sitting there now, which means the Nightcourt has a second war going on underneath the one everyone can see.

VII  The clock

Everyone can see how late it is. Nobody can see who is winding it.

The Nightcourt holds the only prophetic quest in the game. It advances in four stages, and every stage shows up on the map for all players to read. What never shows up is the hand behind it.

I
The nights lengthen
Travel after dark becomes possible for everyone. It is useful, so nobody complains, and nobody investigates.
Where the world stands now
II
The dead do not settle
Named settlements start filing reports. The marked towns are a trail, and the trail points back at somebody's movements.
III
The sun fails for a day
The church stops arguing about the war. Inquisition unlocks, and being at zero standing is no longer the same as being safe.
IV
The endless night
The board turns over. Vampire armies move for the first time. The game does not end here, but everything left to play for changes, and there is not much left of it.

The two kingdoms can stop this. Peace between Andren and Sessau frees the church to look inward, and a church looking inward is the one thing the Nightcourt genuinely fears. But peace has to be declared by someone sitting a throne, and taking a throne takes most of a game.

So the war has to end for the world to survive, and the war is how you win. That is the whole design, in one sentence.
