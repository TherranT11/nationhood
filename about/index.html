About the game
The world

It is nigh the end of the Second Age, and the long-held prophecies are starting to come true. Darkness creeps back into the land. The once-powerful human kingdoms are decadent and bloated, their granaries full and their walls unrepaired. Defeated long ago and long forgotten, clans of orcs begin to stalk the land once more.

Nationhood: Prophecy & Plague is a high fantasy nation simulator. You take a people, a border, and a treasury, and you rule — through politics, armies, and coin. Every other nation on the map is another player doing the same, and the prophecy is watching all of you.

Two sides

Every player stands with the forces of Good or the forces of Evil. The choice is permanent for the age, and it decides who will trade with you, who will answer your call to arms, and what victory even means for you.

The tick

The world advances on a tick every eight hours — three times a day, at 00:00, 08:00 and 16:00 server time. Between ticks you give orders: raise a levy, sow a field, sign a treaty, move an army. Nothing happens the moment you click it. Everything happens at once, when the tick lands.

This is the single most important rule in the game, because it means you never have to be awake at the right moment. Two players who both submit orders before the tick are treated identically, whether one did it eight hours early and the other eight minutes. There is no advantage to setting an alarm for 3am, and no way for a rival to catch you simply by being online when you aren't.

What resolves on every tick
Coin
Taxes collected, upkeep paid, debts called in.
Field
Harvests come in. Populations grow, or starve.
Stone
Construction advances one stage. Sieges progress.
March
Armies move one leg. Battles resolve simultaneously.
Rot
Plague spreads along roads and trade routes.
Omen
The prophecy advances. Everyone sees how far.

Miss a tick and the world does not wait, but it does not abandon you either. Standing orders keep running: your fields are still worked, your garrisons still hold, your caravans still ride. A player who checks in once a day stays competitive. A player who checks in three times a day is simply more responsive to a war.

What you actually do
Rule

Set taxes, manage unrest, hold your court together. A province that hates you is a province that opens its gates.

War

Raise levies, march them, and feed them. Distance is real — an army sent to the far border is gone for days.

Trade

Buy and sell on a live market driven by other players. Embargo an enemy and you may starve them before you fight them.

Faith

Read the omens, fund the temples, and push the prophecy toward your reading of it.

How an age ends

An age runs for roughly six months of real time, and it ends when the prophecy is fulfilled or broken — not when a clock runs out. Both sides can see the omens advancing, so everyone knows roughly how long is left, and everyone can guess what the other side is racing toward.

When the age turns, the map is remade and the Third Age begins. Your nation does not carry over, but your name does: every ruler who held a ward, broke a seal, or died defending one is written into the chronicle that the next age is played on top of.
