Corporate Guide
How to run a corporation in Nationhood
Getting Started

You run a corporation — a business entity that operates within a nation's economy. Unlike political parties, corporations make money through contracts, properties, and financial operations.

  1. Check your Dashboard — the Home tab shows your identity card, finances, properties, workforce, and market stats.
  2. Visit Operations — bid on construction contracts, manage projects, buy equipment, and handle materials.
  3. Buy Property — purchase office buildings or regional HQs from the property marketplace to house your workforce.
  4. Bid on Contracts — government and private construction contracts appear in the Open Contracts pool. Submit competitive bids to win work.

Tip: Your corporation earns market revenue each tick based on your nation's economic health. Keep an eye on GDP growth, urbanization, and inflation.

How Corp Ticks Work

The corp tick runs at the midpoint of each game tick. During a corp tick:

  • Contract bidding windows close and winners are awarded
  • Active projects progress (if properly staffed and supplied)
  • Market revenue is calculated and credited
  • Expenses are deducted (wages, materials, maintenance, debt service)
  • Construction events may fire on in-progress projects
  • Loan payments are processed (both as borrower and lender)
  • Insurance premiums are collected
  • Property condition degrades slightly each tick

Construction Contracts

The core gameplay loop for Construction corporations:

  1. Browse Open Contracts — government and private projects appear in the contracts pool
  2. Submit a Bid — set your price, choose material quality, and allocate labor
  3. Win the Contract — lowest qualified bidder wins (reputation matters for ties)
  4. Supply Materials — allocate concrete, steel, glass, and other materials from your warehouse
  5. Staff the Project — assign workers. Understaffed projects stall.
  6. Complete & Get Paid — payment is the bid price minus penalties for late delivery or low quality

Construction Events: In-progress projects may encounter disruptions (labor strikes, equipment failures, weather damage). These cost money and delay the project. Insurance can cover these costs.

Properties & Buildings

Properties house your workforce and generate passive revenue:

  • Buy from Marketplace — existing buildings at market prices. Lower condition but immediate revenue. +1 market share per 100 employees.
  • Build Custom — commission a new building via Construction Project. Higher quality, better reputation (+2 per 200 employees), but takes time and costs more.
  • Refurbish — restore condition on aging properties to maintain value.
  • Sell — liquidate properties you no longer need.

Finance Corporations

Finance corps operate in three subsectors:

Banking (Loans)

Lend money to other corporations. Borrowers request loans → you review in Deal Flow → set an interest rate → click Offer Loan. Borrower accepts or rejects. Monthly payments come in automatically. Default risk if borrower can't pay.

Investment (Bonds)

Buy government bonds issued by the Minister of Finance. Browse bond offerings in Deal Flow → choose how much to buy → collect monthly coupon payments from the national treasury. Higher coupon = riskier nation. Sovereign default = you lose your principal.

Insurance

Insure construction projects against disruption events. Construction corps click INSURE on their projects → you review in Deal Flow → set premium rate, coverage, and deductible → click Write Policy. Collect premiums monthly. Pay out when disruption events fire.

Purchasing Power

Your cash is worth more or less depending on your nation's economy:

  • Currency Strength — higher = your money buys more. Baseline is 50.
  • Inflation — higher = your money buys less. Erodes purchasing power over time.

The Purchasing Power indicator below your Cash/Debt display shows the real value of your reserves. A weak currency with high inflation means your $200M might only be worth $120M in real terms.

Tips for New Players
  • Buy property first — you need buildings to house workers. No workers = no projects.
  • Start with small contracts — civil engineering contracts are cheaper and less risky than mega projects.
  • Watch your cash flow — wages and maintenance are deducted every tick. Don't overextend.
  • Refurbish aging properties — condition degrades. Below 50% your property loses value fast.
  • Insure big projects — disruption events can cost millions. Insurance is cheaper than eating the loss.
  • Finance corps: diversify — don't put all your capital into one nation's bonds or one borrower's loans.
  • Check the News tab — see what's happening across the world. Political instability = opportunity for some, risk for others.
